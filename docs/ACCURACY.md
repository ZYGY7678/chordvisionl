# Accuracy, measured

Generated by `tests/benchmark.py` and `tests/holdout.py`. Every
number here comes from running the engine; none of it is an estimate.

## How it is measured

The harness renders audio from a chord progression it controls, so the
reference annotation is exact rather than approximate. The engine then
reads only the rendered samples.

Scoring uses Chord Symbol Recall from MIREX: the share of the audio's
**duration** for which the estimated chord matches the reference. It is
reported at five vocabulary levels, because "wrong" is not one thing — an
engine that hears A minor instead of A minor 7 has made a much smaller
error than one that hears F.

| level | what has to match |
|---|---|
| `root` | the root only |
| `majmin` | root, and major vs minor |
| `triad` | root, and the triad type (maj/min/dim/aug/sus) |
| `sevenths` | the full quality |
| `bass` | the full quality and the inversion |

Chord *timing* is scored separately, as boundary precision and recall at a
±250 ms tolerance, because naming chords and placing them are different
skills and an average would hide which one failed.

## The split

`benchmark.py` holds the 24 items the engine's parameters were swept
against. `holdout.py` holds 12 items with different progressions, keys,
tempos and instruments that were **never** used for tuning. A score
measured on the material that chose the parameters is not evidence; the
gap between the two is.

## What drags the numbers down, and why

The weak items are not random; they cluster on two causes.

**A walking bass.** `sevenths_jazz_ii_V_I`, `sevenths_sixths_+7` and
`extended_walking` are the worst items in the set, and all three have a
bass line that moves through the chord rather than holding the root. A D
minor 7 whose bass walks D–F–A–C spends three beats out of four sounding
like F major, because over those beats it *is* an F major triad with a D
somewhere above. The chromagram weighting already holds the bass register
back; what remains is genuine ambiguity in the signal.

**Chords that are the same notes.** C6 is C E G A. A minor 7 is A C E G.
They are the same four pitch classes, and no spectral method distinguishes
them — only musical context does, which is why the engine reports both as
alternatives with their confidences rather than picking one and sounding
certain.

## A negative result, recorded

Using the bass register to reinforce the chord root is an obvious idea and
it does not work. Swept at weights 0.08, 0.15, 0.25 and 0.40 against this
same test set, every non-zero value made every metric worse, and 0.40 cost
11 points of chord recall. The term survives in `decode.py` as
`bass_root_weight`, defaulted to 0.0, with the measurement in its
docstring, so the next person to have the idea can see it was tested
rather than overlooked.

Reproduce with:

```bash
python3 tests/sweep.py --grid '{"bass_root_weight":[0.0,0.15,0.4]}'
```

## The honest caveat

This audio is synthesised. The renderer works hard to be difficult — real
harmonic spectra with inharmonic partials, voicings spread and inverted
across octaves, strums spread in time, drums landing on the chord changes,
reverb smearing one chord into the next, detuned recordings, noise — and
the engine reads nothing but the resulting samples.

It still is not a commercial recording. There are no distorted guitars
whose spectrum shifts with the note, no vocals sliding between pitches, no
bus compression pumping the whole mix. Published systems evaluated on real
annotated music report roughly 80–85% at the majmin level. **Treat the
figures above as an upper bound, not a forecast.**

Running this harness against a real annotated corpus (Isophonics, Billboard,
RWC) is the obvious next step; those datasets ship annotations but not the
audio, which has to be sourced separately.

## Production recognizer note (v2)

The historical measurements above were produced by the original in-repository
engine and must not be interpreted as measured accuracy for the replacement
recognizer. Production analysis now runs the supplied CQT/NNLS/Viterbi engine
through `engine/chordvision_engine/engine_adapter.py`. Its supplied synthetic
self-tests are useful regression checks, but synthetic results do not establish
accuracy on arbitrary commercial recordings. The production end-to-end suite
checks the adapter contract and a known generated progression; a representative
real-song holdout evaluation is still required before making a general accuracy
claim.
